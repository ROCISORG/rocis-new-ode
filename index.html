Outdoor air doesn't stay outside

Most of our exposure to outdoor pollution happens while we are indoors.

We spend most of our time inside, at home, at school and at work. Outdoor air drifts in through windows, doors and small gaps, even in a tight house, so what is outside ends up in the air we breathe inside.

And in southwestern Pennsylvania we share one airshed. When the weather traps pollution, such as during a morning inversion, air gets worse on hilltops and in valleys alike.

The good news

One household can't change the region's air, but it can change the air inside its own home. That is what ROCIS helps participants learn to do.

A network of volunteers, measuring every minute

Each cohort is a group of homes that monitors at the same time, usually for three to four weeks.

Volunteers have measured outdoor air at homes across southwestern Pennsylvania since 2015.

  1. Volunteers download their monitorsEach week they save the readings from the monitor outside their home and send the file to ROCIS.
  2. The files are checked and cleanedBroken lines and repeated readings are removed, and one-minute readings are averaged into 15-minute values.
  3. Each home stays anonymousA home appears only as a random four-digit number and its neighborhood, like 3217 in Squirrel Hill.
